# Working with Another Student

## The 10-minute setup

1. Create a separate ChatGPT conversation for the student and module.
2. Ask them to upload lecture slides, official tutorials, revision sheets and past papers.
3. Ask the course intake prompt for exam date, format, duration and scope.
4. Let the source audit build a syllabus and question inventory.
5. Ask the student how they prefer to learn. Do not assume your preferences transfer.
6. Run a small diagnostic batch and record the baseline.

## Personalise only what needs personalising

Keep the reliable structure: source-first, concept-by-concept teaching, batch practice, detailed marking, error log, honest progress and mock exam. Adapt:
- Plain-language level and language preference.
- Amount of visual explanation.
- Batch size and question difficulty.
- Whether the exam emphasises MCQs, calculations, coding or essays.
- Time available before the exam.
- Accessibility and device constraints.

## Use the student's own sources

Do not reuse your course's answer key, lecturer terminology or question bank for another module. Do not claim a question is official unless it comes from the student's course files.

## Keep records separate

Each student needs a distinct tracker and error log. Never share personal grades, uploaded materials, names, contact details or private notes without permission.

## What to tell the student

This system helps organise learning and practice. It cannot guarantee a grade, replace the lecturer's instructions, or verify learning without the student attempting questions. Encourage the student to check any ambiguous source detail with their instructor.
